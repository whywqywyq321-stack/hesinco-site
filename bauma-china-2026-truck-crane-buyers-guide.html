bauma CHINA 2026 Opens Tomorrow — Here's How to Evaluate a Truck Mounted Crane Supplier

bauma CHINA 2026 runs Nov 24–27 at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre, and special-purpose vehicle makers are using it to court overseas buyers. Whether you walk the halls or source remotely, the same five questions separate a real crane-truck factory from a trading office with a website.

Why this week matters: industry reports around the show describe Chinese special-vehicle manufacturers pushing hard into Southeast Asia, Central Asia, the Middle East and Africa — infrastructure, mining and utility fleets are the demand engine. Translation for buyers: more suppliers are knocking, and the gap between them is widening. Here is the checklist we would use in your seat.

1. Ask for MIIT announcement model numbers, not brochures

In China, every truck design that can be legally sold and registered is filed as an announcement model with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT). Specs are public record and customs paperwork flows from them. If a supplier can quote you a model number — ours run under the LINGYANG (凌扬牌) brand — the design is homologated. If they can't, you're buying a prototype and finding out at the port.

Every truck in our lineup is announcement-registered, China VI, and factory-built:

  • LYP5180JSQ — 18 t GVW, 2 axles, curb 12,810 kg, rated payload 4,995 kg, cargo bed 5,000 × 2,450 mm. The municipal-fleet class.
  • LYP5250JSQ — 25 t GVW, 3 axles, payload 8,070 kg, bed up to 8,000 mm, three Yuchai engines from 180 to 213 kW. The construction-material hauler.
  • LYP5310JSQ — 31 t GVW, 4 axles, payload 8,870 kg, engine options including MC07.34-60 and WP8.350E61. The mining-service and heavy-contracting class.

2. Check whether payload already deducts the crane

The payload figures above are announcement-registered with the crane mounted. When offers from different factories look too good, compare curb weights, not payload claims. A truck quoted with payload that ignores the crane weight will be over its legal GVW the day it rolls off the wharf — and that fine lands on you, not the seller.

3. Ask which crane brands they actually mount

We fit XCMG, Shimei and SANY-Palfinger cranes matched to the chassis class, and quote straight-arm and knuckle-boom configurations side by side so you can compare landed prices. A supplier that only offers one unbranded crane is telling you something about their supply chain.

4. Confirm emission compliance for your jurisdiction before paying

All the models above are China VI diesel, with engine families (Yuchai, Sinotruk MC07, Weichai WP8) that have long parts track records across Africa, Southeast Asia and the Middle East. Configuration is adapted to the destination market at the factory — but the right workflow is: you name the country, we confirm the compliant configuration, then we quote. Not the other way around.

5. Weigh after-sales like it's part of the price

The export story out of bauma CHINA this year is not just sales volume — it's overseas service networks. Parts availability and service reach decide your fleet's uptime, and uptime decides your real cost per ton lifted. Ask any supplier three things: where is the nearest parts stock to my market, what's the response commitment, and who signs off the warranty work.
