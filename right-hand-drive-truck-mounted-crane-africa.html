Right Hand Drive Truck Mounted Crane for Africa & Southeast Asia: What Recent Export Orders Tell You

This week alone, Chinese builders announced a completed HOWO 4×2 right-hand-drive crane truck with a 4-ton XCMG telescopic crane headed for Africa, and a fleet of ISUZU hydraulic crane trucks delivered to a construction company in Laos. A right hand drive truck mounted crane is clearly one of the hottest configurations in China's special-vehicle export trade right now — here is how to spec one properly, with real MIIT announcement numbers.

Two export stories landed within the last few days. A Hubei-based manufacturer finished a HOWO 4×2 RHD cargo truck fitted with an XCMG SQ4-class 4,000 kg telescopic straight boom crane, hydraulic outriggers and a fold-down steel cargo body, now preparing for shipment to Africa. At the same time, ISUZU Vehicles confirmed a contract supplying hydraulic crane trucks to a major construction company in Laos for road, building and infrastructure work. Different brands, same signal: left-side-driving markets are ordering crane trucks in volume, and RHD is the configuration that unlocks them — Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Mozambique, Zambia, Malaysia, Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh and more.

What the recent right hand drive truck mounted crane orders have in common

Strip the press releases down and the Africa/Laos orders share three features. First, a telescopic straight boom crane of 4 to 8 tons class mounted behind the cab — enough to self-load steel, poles, bags and machinery, without needing a separate mobile crane. Second, hydraulic outriggers, because most of these job sites are unpaved. Third, a drop-side cargo body so the same truck still works as a normal delivery vehicle on its non-lifting days. That "one truck, two jobs" logic is exactly why fleet owners in developing markets buy this category instead of a dedicated carrier.

Real announcement specs: the 18 t and 25 t RHD-eligible classes

Our own announcement book mirrors the trend. The LYP5180JSQ is the compact two-axle unit: GVW 18,000 kg, curb weight 12,810 kg, rated payload 4,995 kg (crane included, per the MIIT announcement), cargo bed 5,000 × 2,450 × 400 mm, wheelbase 4,200 or 4,500 mm. It drives into gated compounds and municipal yards that bigger trucks cannot enter — the profile most urban utility contracts in East Africa ask for.

Step up to the LYP5250JSQ, the three-axle workhorse: GVW 25,000 kg, curb weight 16,800 kg, rated payload 8,070 kg, bed 8,000 × 2,450 × 500 mm (a 7,000 mm variant is registered), wheelbases from 2,050+3,550 up to 2,050+5,350 mm, and three Yuchai engines on the announcement — YCS06245-60A (180 kW), YCS06270-60 (199 kW) and YCS06290-61 (213 kW). This is the class that dominates construction-fleet inquiries from East and West Africa: one truck loads, hauls and unloads a full day of material without outside equipment.

Four things to confirm before wiring a deposit on an RHD crane truck

1. Steering side availability. Whether a given chassis can be built right-hand drive depends on the chassis manufacturer's export program. Tell us your destination country first; we confirm the RHD-eligible chassis and the matching announcement model. 2. Crane class vs payload. A bigger crane eats into legal payload — check the rated payload on the announcement, not the brochure. 3. Destination compliance. Like the recent Africa shipment notes correctly state, RHD helps you match traffic direction, but the vehicle must still satisfy your country's registration, roadworthiness and import rules — ask us which emission standard and paperwork we ship with. 4. Outrigger and ground reality. Confirm the crane's load chart conditions and whether your sites need outrigger pads; we can spec the stabilizer layout to match.
