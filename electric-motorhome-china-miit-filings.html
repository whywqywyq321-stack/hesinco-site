Electric Motorhome from China: MIIT's Newest RV Filings Show Where the Market Is Going

China's MIIT published its newest RV announcement batch this week, and the headline repeats a pattern we have watched all year: 22 new motorhome and camper models from 19 manufacturers, with pure-electric motorhomes and new-energy campers taking a growing share even as total filings cool off. If you import RVs, the electric motorhome question is no longer "if" — it is "which platform, and what do I actually get?" Here is the factory-side read, with real announcement data.

The weekly industry coverage of the latest batch is worth a careful read for importers. Travel-trailer filings have now gone four consecutive batches without a single entry, while manufacturers pour resources into self-propelled motorhomes and — increasingly — new-energy campers. Of the newest filings, several are pure-electric or hybrid. At the same time, China's domestic RV shows report prices moving down and younger buyers moving in. Both trends point the same way: the next wave of RV demand, at home and abroad, will be smaller, more energy-efficient and partially or fully electrified.

What a real electric motorhome looks like in the announcement book

We already certify one. The LYP5060XLJKFEV is a registered pure-electric motorhome (纯电动旅居车) on the MIIT announcement: GVW 6,495 kg, curb weight 6,085 kg, overall dimensions 5,995 × 2,530 × 3,350 mm, top speed 100 km/h, wheelbase 3,360 or 3,600 mm. Power comes from two registered permanent-magnet synchronous motors — TZ230XS-HD002 at 165 kW and TZ230XSHD005 at 160 kW. A full living module rides on top of that platform. Battery capacity and charging specifics depend on the battery pack ordered and are confirmed per configuration (contact us for details).

Why does this matter for an importer? An electric motorhome gives you silent camping, zero exhaust inside pitches and rally grounds, and — in a growing list of destination markets — lower import duty or easier registration for zero-emission vehicles. Those policy gaps are exactly what the recent MIIT filing pattern is chasing.

The bridge product: plug-in hybrid motorhomes

Not every market is ready for pure electric. Our LYP5070XLJPHEV1 is a registered plug-in hybrid motorhome: GVW 7,360 kg, curb weight 6,200 kg, overall dimensions 5,995 × 2,550 × 3,330 mm, top speed 95 km/h, wheelbase 3,300 mm, certified in announcement batch 384. Diesel for the highway, electric assist for the campsite — a configuration domestic Chinese shows this season have been promoting heavily as a "new traffic-puller" product. For importers serving long-distance touring markets, it is the low-risk way to put "new energy RV" on your price list today.

The diesel baseline still earns its keep

Electric and hybrid are the trend line, but the workhorses remain. The LYP5066XLJ (GVW 6,495 kg, curb weight 5,900 kg, Isuzu 4HK1 engines at 139–151 kW, top speed 110 km/h) is the classic 6-tonne C-class layout that rental fleets keep reordering. For markets with strict license-tier rules, the 4.5-tonne LYP5046XLJ (GVW 4,495 kg, curb weight 4,080 kg, top speed 145 km/h, SAIC SC20M engines up to 130 kW) fits ordinary driving-license brackets in many countries. And if you need headroom for families, the LYP5130XLJ steps up to 13,495 kg GVW with Weichai WP9H/WP8 power at 257 kW. Cargo-side figures like payload are not separately rated for motorhomes (they are living vehicles, not trucks) — interior fit-out and options are confirmed per order.

How to read the filing trend as an importer

Three takeaways from this week's batch coverage. First, supply is consolidating: fewer, more purposeful filings mean the surviving platforms are the ones worth importing — we are on the right side of that filter, with every model here being a registered announcement vehicle. Second, energy type is becoming a purchase criterion, not a gimmick: quote your market a diesel, a PHEV and a pure-electric side by side and let the duty policy decide. Third, the trailer segment is dormant — if your customers tow, source self-propelled now and revisit trailers later.
