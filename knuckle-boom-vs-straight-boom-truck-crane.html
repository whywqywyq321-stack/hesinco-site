Knuckle boom vs straight boom: the 30-second version

A straight boom (telescopic) crane extends in a straight line. It reaches farther, cycles faster, and is the natural pick for open yards, construction material delivery and loading flatbeds from a distance. A knuckle boom (articulated) crane folds like a finger. It stows compactly behind the cab, works precisely inside tight streets, buildings and rooftops, and can tuck loads close against walls — which is why city utilities and indoor contractors favor it.

Quick rule: open sites and long reach → straight boom; tight urban work and precision placement → knuckle boom. Many fleet owners order both types across their fleet; several of our announcement families support either crane on the same chassis, so you can standardize trucks and vary the cranes.

With a straight boom over the bed, one truck loads and delivers steel, bricks and machinery in a single cycle — the reason this class dominates construction-fleet orders from Africa and the Middle East.
