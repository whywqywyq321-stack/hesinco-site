China's RV Export Boom Is Real — Here's What Importers Should Know Before Ordering

The 28th Shanghai International RV & Camping Show just closed with record overseas-buyer attendance, and the numbers behind it explain why importers from Australia to the Gulf are re-evaluating Chinese motorhomes. Here's the practical version — what you can actually order, with real specs.

The numbers behind the boom

According to Luchangwang (路程网) statistics reported around this month's Shanghai show, China is now Asia's largest RV market and the world's third-largest motorhome manufacturer. RV parts exports passed ¥55 billion in 2025, holding roughly 28–30% of the global market, and combined vehicle-plus-parts overseas shipments exceeded 100,000 units (sets) that year. Domestic registrations grew 24.5% in 2025, with new-energy RVs reaching a 17.49% share — and reporting from this year's show put NEV RV growth at 134% year-on-year for the first seven months of 2026. A ten-department policy package in September 2026 added further momentum.

What does that mean for an importer? Two things: the supply chain is deep and maturing fast, and the factories still standing after this growth wave are the ones worth talking to.

The compliance lesson from mature markets

The most instructive export story this season comes from the Australia–New Zealand market: a leading Chinese RV maker has said publicly that its first shipment required electrical-system rectification to meet local compliance, and that it rebuilt its product development and after-sales process around destination-market standards — later becoming one of the market's top sellers. The lesson isn't "avoid China." It's order from a factory that asks about your destination market before it quotes. Electrical systems, gas installations and road regulations vary hard between markets; the supplier who plans for them at design stage is the one whose container clears without a rectification bill.
