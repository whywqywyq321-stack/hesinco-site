How to Choose a Truck Mounted Crane for Municipal & Utility Work

Six questions decide 80% of the deal: GVW class, payload, bed length, wheelbase, engine and emission compliance. Below we walk through each — with the real specs of three trucks we build, straight from China's MIIT announcement records.

1. Match the GVW class to your job (and your country's road rules)

"Truck mounted crane" covers everything from a light utility truck with a small knuckle boom to a 55-tonne heavy crane carrier. The first filter is gross vehicle weight (GVW) — because your country's bridge and road regulations, not the crane, set the ceiling.

Here is how our own lineup breaks down, all China VI, all with factory-direct pricing:

  • 18 t / 2-axle class — LYP5180JSQ. GVW 18,000 kg, curb weight 12,810 kg, rated payload 4,995 kg, cargo bed 5,000 × 2,450 mm. The nimble choice for municipal fleets: utility repair, street-light maintenance, water works. Two wheelbase options (4,200 / 4,500 mm) let you trade bed length for maneuverability.
  • 25 t / 3-axle class — LYP5250JSQ. GVW 25,000 kg, payload 8,070 kg, bed up to 8,000 × 2,450 mm — the long-bed favorite for construction material delivery. Four wheelbase combinations (2,050 + 3,550 to 2,050 + 5,350 mm) and three Yuchai engine options from 180 to 213 kW.
  • 31 t / 4-axle class — LYP5310JSQ. GVW 31,000 kg, payload 8,870 kg, bed 7,500 × 2,450 mm, engine choices including MC07.34-60 and WP8.350E61. This is the workhorse class for mining-service and heavy municipal contracting.

Rule of thumb: if the truck spends its day inside cities and compounds, stay in the 18–25 t class. If it hauls steel and machinery to remote sites, the 31 t four-axle pays for itself.

2. Do the payload math before you fall in love with a crane

Payload = GVW − curb weight − crane weight − everything you bolt on (toolbox, outrigger pads, water tank). That's why the rated payload figures above (4,995 / 8,070 / 8,870 kg) already account for the crane mounted on the announcement model. When you compare offers from different factories, always ask two questions: what is the curb weight with the crane installed? and is that the announcement-registered figure? If a seller quotes payload without the crane weight deducted, the truck will be illegal on the road the day it arrives.

3. Bed length decides the crane, not the other way around

Municipal buyers usually want a shorter bed (4,500–5,000 mm) for tight streets — the 18 t class is built for exactly that. Construction and mining buyers want 7,500–8,000 mm beds to carry pipe, timber or a mini excavator alongside the crane. Our 25 t and 31 t models offer multiple bed and wheelbase combinations in one announcement family, so you can match the layout without waiting for a new homologation.
